Minh Vo

Van Nhat Minh Vo

I am an Imaging Scientist / Research Associate in the Department of Neuroimaging at King's College London, within the Institute of Psychiatry, Psychology & Neuroscience. At the BRAIN Centre, my work focuses on the acquisition, processing, and analysis of neuroimaging data to support the development of robust imaging biomarkers for brain disorders.

My research connects cardiovascular imaging and neuroimaging through quantitative and multimodal MRI. I am interested in how complementary measurements of tissue structure, function, perfusion and metabolism can improve the characterisation of disease. My work includes MRI acquisition, protocol development, quality control, image processing and reproducible analysis pipelines.

From 2022 to 2025, I undertook doctoral research in Experimental Medicine at the University of Bonn with Prof. Verena Hörr, focusing on cardiovascular MRI. Previously, I studied Radiological Sciences at Tokyo Metropolitan University with Prof. Takako Shirakawa and worked as a research assistant at The Jikei University Hospital. My background also includes MRI applications and product specialist roles at Philips Healthcare, and clinical radiography in Vietnam.

Research interests

My research integrates cardiovascular and neuroimaging, using advanced MRI techniques to investigate structural, functional and metabolic changes in disease. I focus on quantitative biomarkers in preclinical models, including cardiac function, cerebral perfusion and metabolism, with an emphasis on robust and reproducible imaging pipelines.

Metabolism & molecular contrast

Investigating metabolic changes in preclinical models through MRI and spectroscopy.

  • CEST MRI
  • MR Spectroscopy

Brain structure & function

Characterising tissue, cerebral perfusion and functional changes with complementary MRI techniques.

  • Diffusion MRI
  • ASL
  • fMRI

Quantification & computation

Developing quantitative biomarkers and reproducible workflows for imaging acquisition, processing and analysis.

  • Quantitative MRI
